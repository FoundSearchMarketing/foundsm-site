# Slack-Mined Content Pipeline — Executive Messaging Brief

**Source:** Slack-Mined Content Pipeline tab, Q3/Q4 editorial planning sheet (compiled Aug 19, 2026)
**Purpose:** Reframe every pipeline row from a practitioner finding into a business outcome a CMO, VP of Marketing, or CFO-adjacent buyer will act on.
**Audience assumption:** The reader owns a budget they have to defend. They do not care what we tested. They care what it did to cost, risk, or competitive position.

---

## How to use this brief

Every row below has been rewritten against four questions. Writers should be able to answer all four before drafting a word.

| Field | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Exec headline** | Would a CMO stop scrolling for this? |
| **Lens** | Which of the three buying motives this piece pulls on |
| **Outcome owned** | The single business result the piece is allowed to claim |
| **So what (one line)** | The sentence a reader would repeat to their boss |

### The three lenses

Not every idea is a revenue story. Forcing all fourteen into one frame is what makes agency content sound the same. Each row is assigned the lens that actually fits it:

- **Revenue & efficiency** — cost per acquisition, wasted spend, ROAS, qualified volume on flat budget. The default for anything with a performance delta.
- **Risk & control** — signal loss, brand safety, compliance, budget defensibility, loss of control to automation. The right lens for regulated buyers (healthcare, pharma, education) and for anything where the finding is a warning, not a win.
- **Competitive advantage** — what a competitor cannot do yet. Reserved for genuine first-mover material. Overused, it stops meaning anything.

### The reframe rule

> A tactic is not an outcome. A metric is not an outcome. An outcome is what changes on the budget line, the risk register, or the competitive map.

Practitioner framing: *"We tested conversion-optimized bidding on ChatGPT Ads."*
Executive framing: *"A channel your competitors have not budgeted for is currently our cheapest source of qualified leads."*

Same story. Only the second one gets forwarded.

## Tier 1 — Publish first

### 1. ChatGPT Ads early-adopter test

- **Exec headline:** *The Cheapest Qualified Leads We Bought This Quarter Came From a Channel That Didn't Exist in Your Budget*
- **Lens:** Competitive advantage, closed with revenue & efficiency
- **Outcome owned:** Roughly 29% lower cost per lead on non-brand and 21% on brand — inside 12 days of launch.
- **So what:** Your competitors are not bidding here yet, and the entry price is falling fast enough that waiting a quarter costs real money.
- **Why an exec cares:** This is the rare piece that answers "what should I do differently on Monday" with a budget decision rather than a tactic. The 12-day timeframe is the most persuasive number in the whole pipeline — it converts "emerging channel" from a 2027 planning item into a current-quarter reallocation.
- **Proof to lead with:** The speed. Twelve days, two campaign types, both down double digits.
- **What we do not say:** How the bidding was structured. Rule 2 applies hard here — the story is that the window is open, not how we walked through it.
- **Format:** Case study + blog + social, in that order
- **Destination:** `/results/case-studies/` — supersedes AI Max as the lead August AI story
- **CTA:** Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** Client anonymization required; confirm OpenAI beta terms permit publishing **before** drafting, not after
- **Timing:** Within two weeks. This is the only row with a closing window.
- **Source:** Byron Schwartz, #focus-paid-search, Aug 10

### 2. Speed of signal

- **Exec headline:** *Your Bidding Is Optimizing Against Yesterday's Leads*
- **Lens:** Revenue & efficiency, with a risk & control undertone
- **Outcome owned:** Qualified starts up 39% and cost per start down 37% — on flat spend.
- **So what:** The same budget produced materially more qualified pipeline. No incremental spend request, no new channel, no new headcount.
- **Why an exec cares:** "Flat spend" is the phrase that gets this forwarded to finance. Every other efficiency story asks for something. This one asks for nothing and delivers on both sides of the ratio at once — more volume *and* lower cost, which most marketing leaders have been told is a tradeoff.
- **Proof to lead with:** Both metrics moving the right way simultaneously, on an unchanged budget.
- **What we do not say:** Anything about bidding depth. Adam's flag governs this row. The publishable insight is *latency of signal*, not our handling of it. Build from Brittany's award-doc rewrite.
- **Format:** Pillar blog + case study
- **Destination:** September Audience Engineering pillar; proof asset for `/capabilities/data-analytics/server-side-tracking/`
- **CTA:** ROAS Calculator → Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Timing:** September, shipped with the pillar
- **Source:** #gcu-strategy Jul 29; Brittany's Partner Award doc

### 3. Record-low cost per lead across five platforms

- **Exec headline:** *We Added Channels and Acquisition Cost Went Down*
- **Lens:** Revenue & efficiency
- **Outcome owned:** Account-record cost per lead on five platforms simultaneously, including the lowest on Meta since 2017.
- **So what:** Diversification did not dilute performance — it lowered blended acquisition cost, which is the opposite of what consolidation logic predicts.
- **Why an exec cares:** This answers a live boardroom argument. Marketing leaders are under pressure to consolidate spend into the two channels that report best. This is evidence that the pressure is wrong, and it comes from one account rather than a benchmark study, which makes it harder to wave off.
- **Proof to lead with:** Five platforms, one account, all at record lows in the same period.
- **Numbers:** Convert every dollar CPL per the table above. "Lowest since 2017" carries the story without a single dollar sign.
- **Format:** One case study, or five platform-specific posts if the approval cycle stalls
- **Destination:** `/results/case-studies/` — pulls `/industry/higher-education/` forward from Q4
- **CTA:** Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** Account-specific. Anonymize per the March precedent; gut-check with Julie / Matt / Kate.
- **Timing:** Sept–Oct, gated on approval
- **Source:** Byron / Rachel Puls, #gcu-channel-mgmt

### 4. Small budget, hard revenue target

- **Exec headline:** *Fourteen Events, One Ticket Target, a Five-Figure Budget*
- **Lens:** Revenue & efficiency
- **Outcome owned:** A per-event ticket goal delivered against a constrained seasonal budget.
- **So what:** The measurement discipline that works at enterprise scale is what makes a small budget survive a hard number.
- **Why an exec cares:** This is the de-risking piece. It tells a buyer who is not ready for a full engagement that a pilot is a real option — and it proves we take small budgets seriously rather than tolerating them. It is also the only row that ties to a revenue event (tickets sold) instead of a lead.
- **Proof to lead with:** A fixed, public goal and whether we hit it. Ticket targets are unusually concrete for our content — do not soften them into "improved awareness."
- **Format:** Case study
- **Destination:** `/results/case-studies/`; supports the Additional Verticals expansion
- **CTA:** Schedule a conversation
- **Approval:** Client approval required; confirm whether partner platform names can be published
- **Timing:** Q4
- **Source:** Kylie Colquitt, Aug 10

## Tier 2 — Depth and credibility

### 5. The mobile fold test

- **Exec headline:** *We Deleted Content From the Top of the Page and Got a Third More Leads*
- **Lens:** Revenue & efficiency
- **Outcome owned:** Mobile conversion rate 1.95% → 2.58%, a 32% relative lift at 100% confidence.
- **So what:** A page change, not a budget change, produced roughly a third more leads from traffic we were already paying for.
- **Why an exec cares:** Zero incremental media spend is the entire argument. Most leaders' instinct is to buy more traffic; this reframes the cheapest available growth as fixing what the existing traffic lands on. The counterintuitive direction — *less* content won — is what makes it memorable.
- **Proof to lead with:** The confidence level. 100% is rare enough in CRO that it does the credibility work on its own.
- **Format:** How-to blog + before/after social
- **Destination:** Folds into the September Landing Page Benchmarks blog as its lead data point
- **CTA:** Schedule a conversation
- **Approval:** Anonymize the campus and client
- **Timing:** September
- **Source:** Andrew Thomas, #gcu-landing-pages, May 26

### 6. The consent checkbox tradeoff

- **Exec headline:** *The Compliance Win That Cost Us Leads*
- **Lens:** Risk & control
- **Outcome owned:** Opt-in rate rose on both pages; conversion rate fell on one vertical.
- **So what:** Consent design is a deliberate tradeoff between list quality and lead volume — and most teams discover which side they landed on a quarter too late.
- **Why an exec cares:** Regulated buyers — healthcare, pharma, education — are making this exact decision without data. The honesty is the differentiator: an agency willing to publish a test that lost something is an agency you can believe when it says something won.
- **Proof to lead with:** That we published a mixed result at all.
- **Format:** Honest-marketing blog
- **Destination:** Q4 evergreen; supports the testing-discipline POV and the `/industry/healthcare-pharma/` narrative
- **CTA:** Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** Anonymize
- **Timing:** Q4
- **Source:** Maria Escobedo, Apr 20

### 7. Account-wide content exclusions, including PMax

- **Exec headline:** *You Cannot See Where Half Your PMax Budget Runs. Here's How to Take That Back.*
- **Lens:** Risk & control
- **Outcome owned:** Brand-adjacency exposure and low-quality placement spend, both reducible through account-level controls.
- **So what:** Brand safety in automated inventory is a settings decision your team can make this week, not a platform limitation you have to accept.
- **Why an exec cares:** Brand safety is a board-level word. This is the one row where the risk is reputational rather than financial, which means it reaches an audience the efficiency pieces do not. Problem-first framing per CEO direction — open with the exposure, not the feature.
- **Format:** Tactical blog + LinkedIn carousel
- **Destination:** Q4 evergreen
- **CTA:** Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** None significant
- **Timing:** Q4
- **Source:** Logan Hartman, Aug 13

### 8. AI Max, honestly

- **Exec headline:** *AI Max Changed What Your Ads Say. Nobody Told You.*
- **Lens:** Risk & control, with a competitive edge
- **Outcome owned:** Text-expansion asset deletions — brand-approved messaging removed from live campaigns without notification.
- **So what:** Handing message control to automation is a brand governance decision, not a performance setting, and it is currently being made by default.
- **Why an exec cares:** A CMO who signed off on messaging does not expect a platform to edit it. This reframes an AI feature review — a topic they would skip — into a question about who controls the brand's words. It also gives Found a defensible position on automation that is neither hype nor refusal.
- **Proof to lead with:** The deletions and Google's own explanation of them.
- **Format:** Practitioner review blog
- **Destination:** Replaces and deepens the planned August AI Max blog; supports `/capabilities/ai-automation/`
- **CTA:** Newsletter sign-up → Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** Keep client references generic
- **Timing:** September
- **Source:** Logan / Adam, #focus-paid-search

### 9. Quora as an AI-citation play

- **Exec headline:** *Being Cited by AI Is Replacing Being Found by Search*
- **Lens:** Competitive advantage
- **Outcome owned:** Cited answers averaging 500+ words and meaningful engagement — hold performance claims until client results land.
- **So what:** When your buyers ask an assistant instead of a search engine, the only visibility that exists is being the source it quotes.
- **Why an exec cares:** This is the successor question to "how do we rank." Most leaders know AI search is coming for their traffic and have no plan; this is a concrete first move rather than a trend piece. It also feeds our own LLM-discovery flywheel, which makes it strategically double-duty.
- **Format:** Results-when-ready blog
- **Destination:** LLM-discovery flywheel (Strategic Priority 1)
- **CTA:** Newsletter sign-up
- **Approval:** Hold for client results; anonymize
- **Timing:** When results exist — do not force this to a date
- **Source:** Adam / Logan / Brittany, Jun 24

### 10. Competitor ad and landing page analysis with AI

- **Exec headline:** *See a Competitor's Entire Funnel in an Afternoon*
- **Lens:** Competitive advantage
- **Outcome owned:** A repeatable workflow that compresses competitive teardowns from weeks to hours.
- **So what:** Competitive intelligence stops being an annual agency deliverable and becomes something your team can run before every budget conversation.
- **Why an exec cares:** Giving away the workflow is the point — it demonstrates capability more convincingly than describing it, and the workflow itself is the IP per CEO direction. High-intent readers who run it and find it valuable arrive at the sales conversation pre-sold.
- **Format:** Step-by-step how-to
- **Destination:** Tools & Custom Products page; evergreen
- **CTA:** Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** None — strip client keywords from all examples
- **Timing:** Q4
- **Source:** Brian Walker, Jun 29

## Tier 3 — Positioning and IP

### 11. Building the AI-augmented agency, in public

- **Exec headline:** *What Should Happen to Your Agency Fee When the Agency Runs on AI*
- **Lens:** Competitive advantage
- **Outcome owned:** Capability, not client data — performance-review agents, anomaly reporting, landing page auto-classification, keyword diagnosis.
- **So what:** AI should buy you more senior strategic attention, not a smaller invoice from the same work done faster.
- **Why an exec cares:** Every marketing leader is being asked by their CFO why agency fees have not fallen. This gives them the answer, and it positions Found on the side of that answer rather than defending against it. Publishing in public — before we are asked — is the differentiator.
- **Format:** Flagship blog series + LinkedIn
- **Destination:** Anchors `/capabilities/ai-automation/` and the custom-tools grid on the Tools page
- **CTA:** Schedule a conversation
- **Approval:** Frame as capability, never client data
- **Timing:** Kick off Q4, ongoing
- **Source:** Julie, #fsm-ai, Apr 23

### 12. The strategy levers framework

- **Exec headline:** *The Three to Ten Levers That Actually Move an Account*
- **Lens:** Revenue & efficiency, framed as control
- **Outcome owned:** A named framework for auditing whether an account is being managed or merely reported on.
- **So what:** It gives a marketing leader a way to tell the difference between an agency pulling levers and an agency sending dashboards.
- **Why an exec cares:** This is the single most useful thing we can hand a prospect who is unhappy with their current agency but cannot articulate why. Naming the framework makes it portable — it gets repeated in rooms we are not in.
- **Format:** Agency-philosophy piece
- **Destination:** `/our-approach/` refresh candidate; named IP per CEO direction
- **CTA:** Revenue Engineering Assessment
- **Approval:** Internal framing approval (Adam's Innovation initiative)
- **Timing:** Q4
- **Source:** #cintas-levers, Aug 13–14

### 13. Startup Study Hall — Before You Spend Another Dollar on Marketing

- **Exec headline:** *Before You Spend Another Dollar on Marketing*
- **Lens:** Revenue & efficiency
- **Outcome owned:** A test-plan template that establishes measurement before spend rather than after.
- **So what:** The most expensive marketing mistake is not a bad channel — it is spending a full budget before you can tell what worked.
- **Why an exec cares:** Different audience from the rest of the pipeline — founders and first marketing hires — but the same argument that anchors the enterprise work. It is a wide-top-of-funnel asset that recruits people into our worldview early.
- **Proof to lead with:** The one-sheeter test plan itself. The asset is the argument.
- **Format:** Lead magnet + blog + email series + event recap
- **Destination:** New gated asset for `/resources/`
- **CTA:** Download the test plan → email nurture
- **Approval:** None
- **Timing:** Align with the workshop date
- **Source:** #startupstudyhall-2026-planning, Aug 12

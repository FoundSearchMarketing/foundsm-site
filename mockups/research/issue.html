The pipeline got quieter the month the dashboards cheered.

Efficiency went up. The conversations that turn into revenue did not. This is the note I would hand a director before the next forecast meeting.

Julie WarneckeFounder and CEOThe public letter from the November issue

I sat in a review last week where every chart was a relief. Cost was down. Volume was up. The person running the media had the kind of month you can put in a board appendix without flinching.

Then the person who owns the number spoke, and the room changed temperature.

The leads were arriving. The conversations were not. Fewer of them made a second meeting. The ones that did were smaller, earlier, and easier to lose. Nobody had been careless. The account had done the thing the platforms reward. It had gotten more efficient at producing the thing the dashboard counts.

I have watched this for twenty years. It still fools smart people, because the number that improved is a real number. It is just not the number the business is on the hook for.

Cheap is a clue. It is not a result.

What I care about, from the seat you are in, is whether next quarter’s revenue got more likely. Not whether the auction got cheaper. A lower cost is a clue about what the account just went and found. It is not, by itself, news the year can use.

The letter is yours, here. What comes by email is the part I don’t post.

  • The Loop note, on where qualified demand thinned
  • Questions I’m asking in the room right now

Once a month. From me. Nothing else gets added to it. The week’s finding lives on its own, as a Signal.

On the accounts where we let the cheap volume go, two things happened that do not look like a win on a Monday. The lead count dipped. The pipeline sales would actually work got steadier, and the revenue we could trace to those conversations held. The month looked worse where most teams report, and better where the year gets decided.

I am not going to give you the speech about vanity metrics. You have heard it. I care about the moment a good operator gets praised for a result the business pays for later. That praise is how the next quarter gets set wrong. The room claps, the target gets copied forward, and twelve weeks later everyone is confused that a “better” account produced a thinner business.

A few things I keep coming back to. None of them is a setting.

The model learns whatever the room applauds. If the room applauds a lower cost, the account will go find more of whatever is inexpensive. Inexpensive traffic is rarely the buyer with a real problem and a calendar. It is usually someone earlier, smaller, or passing through. The model is not mistaken. It is obedient. It will keep bringing you the thing you thanked it for.

Sales capacity is a number, the same as budget. Pour more leads into a team that can only hold so many serious conversations, and you did not create demand. You created a queue. Queues make a good lead look ordinary, because no one reached them while they were still warm. I would rather fund the conversations the team can actually have than buy a pile they will apologize for in March.

And the line that wins on an easy click is often the line that asked the least. The work that names the risk a director is actually carrying gets fewer hands in the air and better rooms. I would rather have the rooms. A click is cheap company. A second meeting is the business showing up.

I want to be plain about the rest. We like this work. We like the conversation after the deck is closed, when someone asks what it actually meant. That is the job. The platforms will keep inventing faster ways to buy more of something. Someone in the room has to keep asking whether that something is the business. We can sit in that conversation with you. The letter is not a pitch for the seat. It is the conversation.

What you just read is public, the whole of it. The rest of the November issue is shorter, and it stays in the envelope: a note on where qualified demand thinned, the questions I am asking in reviews right now, and a few things I noticed that did not earn a chart. It arrives once. Then I leave you alone.

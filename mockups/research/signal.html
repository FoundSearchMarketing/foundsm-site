Week of November 2, 2026 · Paid media

The accounts that eased off in October kept the revenue.

Where we bought less of the cheap volume, the quarter’s revenue held. The dashboards were the last to agree.

The Found teamOne finding from the weekPublic the week we saw it

We spent the first half of October buying what the platforms were eager to sell: more leads, a lower cost, charts that looked newly tidy. On three accounts we then did the less comfortable thing. We stopped paying for the volume sales had quietly stopped working.

Lead count came down. That part showed up immediately, and it is the part a nervous week will stare at. The part that showed up later is the one that matters. The conversations that remained were the ones a rep would book a second meeting for. Revenue we could tie back to those accounts held. On two of them it improved, because the team stopped splitting its week across people who were never going to buy.

The dashboards were the last to agree. They usually are.

Nothing about this is a trick, and it is not a switch we would hand you on a slide. It is a decision about what the business will tolerate looking worse at, for a month, in order to keep the number the year actually depends on.

If you only read the platforms, October looked like a step back on the accounts where we eased off, and like a win on the accounts where we did not. The forecast read it the other way around. We trusted the forecast. The month ended closer to the revenue the business had already promised than to the efficiency the auction was offering.

We will keep publishing these as we see them. One finding, the week we see it, short enough to read between meetings. Julie’s letter is the monthly issue. This is not that. This is the note from the week.

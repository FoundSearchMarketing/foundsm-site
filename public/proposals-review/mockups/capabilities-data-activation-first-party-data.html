Capabilities

You already own the data that would fix your targeting.

Who bought, what they paid, who churned, which leads never answered the phone. It is all sitting in your CRM. In most accounts none of it reaches the platforms deciding where your budget goes, so the algorithms optimize on the thinnest possible version of the truth.

Talk about your data stack

Third-party data is a rental. Your first-party data is the only durable advantage you have.

Every advertiser in your category can buy the same audience segments from the same vendors. None of them can buy your customer list, your margin data, or your close rates. That asymmetry only pays off if the data actually moves.

Three systems that need to talk to each other

Most of the work is not analysis. It is plumbing that holds up.

Salesforce, Dynamics, HubSpot, or something built in-house a decade ago. We map lead stages to what they actually mean commercially, then decide which ones are worth sending back. Not every stage deserves to be a conversion, and treating them all equally is how accounts end up optimizing toward the easiest stage rather than the profitable one.

BigQuery, Looker, Funnel, or a set of spreadsheets doing more work than anyone admits. Where multiple sources and multiple CRMs are in play, the warehouse becomes the place truth gets reconciled before anything is sent anywhere. Skipping that step is how two dashboards end up disagreeing in a board meeting.

Google, Microsoft, Meta, and LinkedIn all accept first-party signal, and all of them handle it differently. Offline conversion imports, customer match audiences, and value rules each have their own match rates, latency, and privacy constraints. Getting this wrong quietly degrades performance rather than throwing an error.
